A channel entry can show a member count, changes over seven or thirty days, an activity band and recent posts. Start by identifying the question each field helps answer, then record when you observed it.

This guide follows TGsearch's current pages and data handling. The calculation table uses hypothetical numbers so you can reproduce the method.

## Identify the metric before comparing it

| Entry information | What to record | Useful follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel subscribers or group members | The directory's recorded size | Check the current count on Telegram |
| Bot monthly active users | Interpret usage according to the field label | Check its function and launch link |
| Member history | Date range, samples and direction | Review content and distribution during that period |
| Activity band | The displayed activity category | Read recent posts and their dates |
| Post views | Publication and observation times | Check forwards, message type and context |

Find candidates in the [channel directory](/en/channel/), then open their entries to collect these details. A shared record makes it easier to compare several candidates.

## Work through member increases and growth rates

Suppose two channels have starting and ending samples covering the same seven-day observation window.

| Example channel | Starting count | Ending count | Increase | Growth relative to the start |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 1,000 | 1,100 | 100 | 10% |
| B | 10,000 | 10,100 | 100 | 1% |

Subtract the starting count from the ending count to obtain the increase. Divide that increase by the starting count for the growth rate. For A, `(1,100 - 1,000) / 1,000 = 10%`.

Record the increase when you want to observe newly added followers. Include the rate when relative change matters to your question. Leave a clear gap in the record when the starting count is zero or unknown.

## Attach an observation window to each comparison

TGsearch's detail charts calculate changes from recorded samples. They use the latest sample as the endpoint and look for a historical sample at or before the relevant period boundary. The starting sample can therefore be older than exactly seven or thirty days before the endpoint.

Check the available history for each entry. A recently added entry and one with several historical samples can support different observation periods. When a chart changes sharply, examine the corresponding dates and gaps between samples before interpreting the movement.

### Working with incomplete history

Record the current count and the missing period, then return when more history is available. Meanwhile, read recent posts to check their topics, publishing schedule and original links. A period change calculation requires a starting observation.

## Keep post views in context

Choose posts published at similar times and record their content types, views and your observation time. Tutorials, announcements and short updates serve different purposes, so keep those distinctions in your notes.

Telegram describes view counts as approximate. Forwarded copies contribute to the total, and a return visit after an interval may be counted again. Review views alongside publication time and forwarding context. [Telegram's view count explanation](https://telegram.org/faq_channels)

When member counts change, look for relevant publishing, events or distribution during the same period. Record confirmed events separately from explanations that still need evidence.

## Keep a record you can revisit

| Record | Suggested contents |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | Username and TGsearch entry URL |
| My purpose | Content I want to follow or a task I want to complete |
| Observation | Date of this review |
| Data range | Current count, history dates and sample count |
| Content evidence | Original links to a few relevant posts |
| Gaps | Missing history, descriptions or unavailable links |
| Follow-up | A review date and questions to check |

Use this record alongside the [rankings](/en/rank/) to connect new data with your observations. For help building the initial shortlist, read the [channel discovery guide](/en/guides/find-telegram-channels/).
