Telegram channels deliver announcements and content updates, groups support conversation, and bots handle tasks such as lookups, submissions and reminders. When browsing TGsearch, choose a type based on what you want to do.

## Choose by your task

| What you want to do | Where to start | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read news, tutorials or project updates | Channel | Recent posts, sources and update dates |
| Ask questions or exchange experience | Group | Rules, discussion topics and posting permissions |
| Look up information, submit details or receive a result | Bot | Instructions, buttons and requested information |
| Publish announcements with reader comments | Channel with a linked discussion group | Whether comments are enabled and who moderates them |

For example, a photography learner could read a tutorial channel, discuss a shooting problem in a group or use a bot with a lookup function. Check the actual features before choosing an entry.

## Channels for ongoing reading

A channel's main message feed is published by administrators or authorized bots. Subscribers receive its updates. Check recent posts to see whether the subject suits you and whether the links work.

Public channels have usernames and public links. Private channels require an invitation or another permitted access route. Channel subscribers have no upper limit. See the [Telegram channel FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq_channels).

A channel can link a discussion group to add a comment button below posts. Comments also appear in that group, where they need moderation. See [Telegram's channel comment explanation](https://telegram.org/blog/filters-anonymous-admins-comments#channel-comments).

## Groups for questions and discussion

Members can post, reply and share material when permissions allow. Owners can configure posting rights, administrators and rules. Telegram describes groups with up to 200,000 members in its [official FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq#q-who-is-telegram-for).

Read the name, description and rules before joining. If you cannot post, check for verification instructions, a mute or posting restrictions. Check the available message history inside the group too.

Before asking a question, search existing discussions. Include the background and what you have already tried so other members can respond.

## Bots for specific tasks

A bot processes messages or button actions through software. It may offer lookups, forms or reminders. Read its instructions before using buttons or commands. See the [Telegram bot introduction](https://core.telegram.org/bots).

TGsearch submissions provide a concrete example. Open this site's bot from the [submission guide](/en/enroll/), send a public link, check the details and submit. The review result arrives by private message.

For other bots, check the service provider and the information requested. Private messages sent to a bot are processed by its software. In groups, message access also depends on privacy mode and administrator permissions. See [Telegram's bot privacy-mode documentation](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#privacy-mode).

You can stop or block a bot in Telegram if you no longer want its messages.
