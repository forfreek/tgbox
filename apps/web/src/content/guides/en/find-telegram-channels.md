Start by writing down what you want from a channel. You might want to learn a Telegram feature each week, follow software releases or read original reporting. That purpose gives you specific things to check when several channels look promising.

This workflow uses TGsearch's categories, entry pages and rankings to build a repeatable shortlist. The two directory examples were checked on November 15, 2026. Entry information can change as public profiles are updated.

## Build a small shortlist

Choose a category close to your purpose, select a few candidates and record each username alongside your reason for considering it. Usernames help distinguish entries with similar names. Review the different ranking views when recent changes matter to your search. [Channel categories](/en/channel/) · [Rankings](/en/rank/)

Public channels can also be found by username in Telegram search. The Posts tab supports public post search. Telegram initially released that feature to Premium users in July 2025. Check your current client's availability and prompts. [Telegram channel FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq_channels) · [Public post search announcement](https://telegram.org/blog/post-search-story-albums-and-more)

## Read the entry with your purpose in mind

| Entry information | Question it helps answer | Follow-up check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Username and description | Have I found the intended channel and topic? | Confirm its identity, links and purpose on the original page |
| Language | Can I comfortably read it? | Look for mixed languages in recent posts |
| Recent posts and dates | What has it published lately? | Open posts for their full context |
| Member count and history | How has its recorded size changed? | Check the chart's date range, samples and gaps |
| Activity label | Which activity band does the directory display? | Review actual posts and their dates |

An automatic translation can help you understand the topic. Verify usernames, URLs, payment conditions and feature limits against the original page. Descriptions come from external public information, so claims such as “official” or “best” also need independent checking.

## Work through two directory examples

### Learning Telegram features

The Telegram Tips entry has an English-language description about Telegram features. If you want practical guidance, choose recent posts relevant to your device or task. Open the original post, check its instructions and scope, and record whether you could complete the task. That gives you a concrete reason to follow the channel. [Telegram Tips entry](/en/detail/telegramtips/)

### An entry with an empty source description

When checked for this guide, Cocoon had an empty original description. Its name, category and member count provided some context. To establish whether it fits your purpose, open the original channel and review its recent posts and linked project information. Mark the candidate as awaiting verification until you have enough evidence to decide. [Cocoon entry](/en/detail/cocoon/)

These examples provide a checking path for both available and missing descriptions. Keep notes of what you have confirmed and what still needs evidence.

## Compare posts within a consistent observation window

Choose a few posts published at similar times. Record their topics, sources, views and usefulness to you. For channels with different schedules, observe a week or a complete publishing cycle. Assess a long tutorial and a short news update according to what you want each to provide.

Telegram includes views from forwarded copies in a post's count and describes view counts as approximate. It also explains that a return visit after an interval may count again. Record your observation date, the post's publication date and whether the content was forwarded when comparing views. [Telegram's view count explanation](https://telegram.org/faq_channels)

Read member trends alongside their date range. A sudden increase or decline deserves a check of activity, distribution and subsequent changes during that period. Leave the question open when the available history is too short.

## Keep a record you can revisit

Copy this checklist and write a short answer for each candidate.

- What do I want to learn or follow, and which posts serve that purpose?
- Can I read the content and use the linked resources?
- How much attention goes to original material, reposts and promotions?
- Do the description, recent posts and original page agree?
- What information needs more evidence, and how will I check it?
- After an observation period, is this channel still worth following?

Save the shortlist and revisit it using the same criteria. If you need conversation or automated tools, explore how groups and bots work alongside channels. [Channels, groups and bots guide](/en/guides/channels-groups-bots/)
